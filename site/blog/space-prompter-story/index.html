How I built Space Prompter in four days

My team needed a teleprompter that did not fight us on set. So I made one, with Claude, between 31 October and 3 November. Here is the real version history, wrong turns included.

31 Oct – 3 Nov 2026 · 118 commits · by Shovon Arifin

The problemEvery shoot, the teleprompter was the weak link

My team has used teleprompter apps for years, and they always came with something: sync problems, Android vs iOS compatibility, paid subscriptions. We also used the Neewer teleprompter app, whose Bluetooth pedal only works with Neewer's own software.

None of it was a disaster. It was a steady tax on every shoot. So I decided to build the one we actually wanted.

The ideaOne page, one job

The first brief was small on purpose. Paste a script. Press play. Text scrolls past a guide line. That is it.

  • No account, no install. A single web page. Scripts stay in the browser.
  • Built for the camera. Mirror and flip on their own axes, because a prompter in front of a lens shows the text reversed.
  • A second screen. The reading view pops into its own window, so it can sit on an external monitor while the controls stay on the main display.
  • Keyboard first. Space to play and pause, arrows for speed. No hunting for buttons mid-take.

31 October · v1The first version was ugly and nearly broken

Version one was a single HTML file. Full-width red button, big empty text box, a scrolling stage. I put it on my own server the same day, then tested it on real devices and found the basics were wrong.

Three bugs showed up in the first live test:

  • No viewport tag. On a phone, the page did not scale to the screen.
  • No character encoding. The arrow symbols in the shortcut hints turned into garbage characters.
  • Play did nothing. The code reached for an element that was never in the page, crashed, and the Space key and the button both died with it.

Each one was a few lines to fix. But it was a useful reminder: the first version always lies about being done until it runs on a real device.

1 November · v1.xRedesigns, and one I reverted

Day two was mostly interface. I rebuilt the control window with frosted-glass bars, tried a heavier "liquid glass" look, and then threw it away and went back to the cleaner baseline. The glass kept making the text underneath bleed through the bar. I patched it several times before finding the real cause: a stacking-context bug, not an opacity problem.

Two things from this day survived into the final app. First, one key does three jobs: tap Space to play and pause, double-tap to go back a paragraph, hold to jump to the start. Second, the Bluetooth pedal. The Neewer pedal stays locked to Neewer's app, so instead of reverse-engineering it, I added a learn mode: press the key your pedal sends, and it acts like Space. Any pedal that sends a key press works.

2 November · v2.0 → v3.0The hard part was keeping two screens in step

The second-screen idea sounded simple and was the single hardest thing in the project. The stage window has to scroll exactly like the control window, at the same wrap width, with no stutter. I went through several attempts: batching updates (too choppy), throttling (fuzzy, stepped motion), a guessed 1280px width (wrong), until the control window was made to read the real width of the stage window and scale to match.

That unlocked v2.0, a dual-monitor control panel, and the day's other big change: it stopped being the "MGD Teleprompter" and became Space Prompter, because the spacebar runs the whole thing. By the end of the day it also had single-monitor, tablet and phone modes.

3 November · v3.xPhone as remote, fade the rest, and shipping

The last day added QR pairing: scan a code on your computer and your phone becomes a remote or a second screen, with the script synced live. It runs through a tiny relay service, and the link is encrypted, so the relay cannot read your script. I also added a Fade others slider, which dims every paragraph except the one you are reading, a demo-text button, rotate and flip controls, and installable-app support.

What I would tell youWhat I took from it

  • Start smaller than you think. The whole idea fit in three boxes. Everything else was added after real use.
  • Test on a real device on day one. Three basic bugs only showed up on a phone.
  • Fix causes, not symptoms. The glass bar bug took several patches until I looked for the actual cause.
  • Revert freely. The liquid-glass look looked impressive and made the tool worse. Undoing it was the right call.

Where it stands. Space Prompter is live, free, and my team has used it on shoots. Native iOS and Android apps are still "coming soon", and for now you install it from the browser. If you try it, tell me what is missing.
